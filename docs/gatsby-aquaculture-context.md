# Gatsby Aquaculture Context - Why a Lake Economy

This note gives agents the real-world inspiration for the Lake Economy game.

## Source case

The game world is inspired by the Gatsby Africa Aquaculture Program. The case involved an effort to build a competitive and environmentally responsible aquaculture sector across Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, and Rwanda.

The case is useful for the website because it is naturally systemic. It includes:

- fish farms,
- feed mills,
- hatcheries,
- smallholder suppliers,
- market access,
- local banks and private capital,
- jobs and poverty reduction,
- import substitution,
- Lake Victoria ecosystem protection,
- coordination across multiple parts of the value chain.

The homepage game should not claim to simulate the real Gatsby program. It should be a stylized toy economy inspired by the case.

## Core story

The aquaculture sector was stuck because the parts of the system constrained each other.

A simplified version:

- Fish farms could not compete well with imported fish.
- Poor feed quality limited fish growth.
- Weak farm economics reduced demand for feed and services.
- Missing market links made scaling harder.
- Investors could not solve the problem by funding one node alone.

Gatsby's approach was to coordinate across related nodes. Funding feed improvements and fish farms together helped raise yields. Higher production created demand for more feed, market links, and private capital. The system moved toward a self-sustaining industry.

## Why this is good for the game

The case naturally supports the game's teaching goal:

```text
The effect of funding one entity depends on the surrounding system.
```

For example:

- A fish farm may have high local prosperity intensity but weak scale unless feed and markets improve.
- A feed mill may look less directly impactful but can unlock fish farm growth.
- Cold chain infrastructure may not look glamorous but can determine whether farm output reaches markets.
- A high-payoff lakefront industry may create jobs while damaging lake health.

This creates a richer game than simply ranking firms by raw scores.

## Suggested investable entities

Use six investable entities in the MVP:

1. Scalable fish farm
2. Steady fish farm
3. Feed mill
4. Hatchery
5. Cold chain / market access
6. Lakefront industry

Do not make the regulator investable in the first version. Regulation can appear later as a Paper 2 policy mode.

Imports can appear as visible competitive pressure, but do not need to be investable in the first version.

## Suggested visual cues

### Scalable fish farm

Energetic entrepreneur, expanding ponds, visible workers, high upside, high risk.

### Steady fish farm

Stable ponds, more modest owner, lower risk, lower scalability.

### Feed mill

Industrial but clean enough, trucks or sacks of feed, a bottleneck node.

### Hatchery

Small facility, tanks or young fish, productivity lever for farms.

### Cold chain / market access

Refrigerated storage, market stall, logistics route, link between production and buyers.

### Lakefront industry

Factory or processing plant near the lake. Financially attractive and job-creating, but potentially harmful to lake health.

## Interaction links

The hidden system should include complements and trade-offs.

Suggested links:

- Feed mill helps fish farms scale.
- Hatchery helps fish farms produce more reliably.
- Cold chain helps fish farms and feed mills by improving market access.
- Fish farms increase demand for feed and cold chain.
- Lakefront industry improves local prosperity but damages lake health.
- Lakefront industry can hurt fish farms through lake quality, land, labor, or capital competition.
- Strong local production reduces import pressure.

These links can be represented through off-diagonal terms in the hidden response system.

## Tone

The game should be charming but not simplistic.

Avoid a cartoon morality play. The lakefront industry should not be an evil villain. It can create jobs and payoff while damaging lake health. The feed mill should not automatically be good in every scenario. Different generated lakes should make different choices attractive.

## What not to overclaim

- Do not say the game is Gatsby's actual model.
- Do not imply all aquaculture is automatically environmentally good.
- Do not imply one actor can solve the whole economy alone.
- Do not present the player as a planner.
- Do not treat local leaders as passive recipients. The story should start from an invitation by local leaders.
