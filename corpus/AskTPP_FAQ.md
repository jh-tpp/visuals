## 1. Purpose and answer-layer architecture

This document is designed to become a higher-priority answer layer for AskTPP rather than
another undifferentiated set of corpus chunks. The proposed serving order is:

1. Match the user’s question against each approved canonical question and its approved alternate phrasings.
2. When there is a strong match, use the approved short or fuller answer as the backbone, with minimal variation and only context-sensitive tailoring.
3. Retrieve passages from the underlying publications to substantiate, qualify, or extend that backbone.
4. Use ordinary corpus retrieval only when no approved FAQ entry adequately covers the question.
5. Ask a clarifying question when the correct answer depends materially on the user’s role, goals, constraints, or intended use.

For broad practical questions, the default clarification is:

> I can give you a much more useful answer if I know where you are coming from. Are you
> approaching this as an asset owner or wealth holder, an advisor, a researcher, a CSP colleague
> or participant, a philanthropist, or someone exploring investor impact more generally?

## 2. Editorial evidence convention

Each entry separates three kinds of statements:

- **Directly supported facts** — claims stated in a publication or current official institutional source.
- **Synthesis** — a reasoned connection across sources. Synthesis should not be presented as a
  quotation or as something every source explicitly says.
- **Editorial or institutional judgment** — choices about naming, hierarchy, institutional
  relationships, recommendation, or answer policy that require human confirmation.

Important default rules:

- “Total Portfolio Project” or “TPP” refers to Jonathan Harris’s independent public-good research
  activity. It is not currently a legal entity.
- Do not infer that a TPP publication is a CSP publication. Check the title page, acknowledgements,
  publisher, and stated affiliations for each item.
- *The Impact Frontier* is an SSRN working paper under development. Its empirical estimates are
  preliminary and should not be represented as final or decision-ready.
- The one-page *Shifting the Frontier* preview is explicitly preliminary and says not to cite or
  circulate without permission.
- Similar terminology across publications does not establish equivalence. In particular, “impact
  returns” is used in related but not identical ways in *The Impact Frontier* and the TPP case-study
  report.
- AskTPP should not present itself as a legal, tax, financial, or investment adviser, or as an official
  spokesperson for CSP, CCSP-HSG, the University of St.Gallen, MIT, or any other institution.

## 4. Audience-routing guide

- **Asset owner or wealth holder:** begin with goals and financial needs; then use the impact,
  wealth-management, multicapital, or systemic materials according to the problem.
- **Advisor or wealth manager:** begin with client-goal elicitation and an impact-aware investment
  policy statement; then use the investor-impact mechanisms and relevant implementation guides.
- **Researcher:** begin with the conceptual distinction between company and investor impact, then
  move to *The Impact Frontier* and its limitations.
- **Philanthropist:** begin with goals, opportunity cost, and coherence across grants, catalytic
  investments, and conventional investments.
- **CSP colleague or participant:** identify the specific program, guide, or research question before
  choosing a starting source.
- **General explorer:** begin with *The Investor’s Guide to Impact* and the goals-based guide before
  moving to technical papers.

## REL-03 — What is The Impact Frontier?

**Canonical question:** What is The Impact Frontier?

**Alternate phrasings**
- What does the Impact Frontier paper do?
- What is an impact frontier in portfolio theory?
- What are contribution multipliers?
- What is the return-impact trade-off?

**Audience:** Researchers, asset owners, technically interested users

**Why a user might ask:** Users need a nontechnical explanation of a highly technical working paper and its scope.

**Match priority:** High

**Answer mode:** Direct answer

**Clarifying question, if needed:** Ask whether the user wants intuition, equations, empirical results, portfolio implications, or limitations.

**Suggested short answer**

*The Impact Frontier* is a working paper that models one channel of investor impact: how a sustained marginal portfolio tilt changes firms’ capital and external outcomes in equilibrium. It derives a frontier between certainty-equivalent financial return and contribution-adjusted portfolio impact return.

**Suggested fuller answer**

The paper starts from a supply-and-demand view of capital markets. An investor shifts the amount of
capital they are willing to supply to particular assets. Other investors may offset part of that shift,
firms may respond by changing the amount of capital they use, and capital can be reallocated across
the system. A contribution-multiplier matrix summarizes those equilibrium responses.

The paper then combines the capital changes with outcome intensities to construct ‘impact returns’:
expected outcome change per dollar of position value. These are outcome units per dollar, not
expected financial returns. The Impact Frontier is the set of marginal portfolio tilts that produce the
largest portfolio impact return for a given certainty-equivalent return cost, or the lowest return cost
for a target impact return.

The current calibration uses U.S. public equities, five-year windows, and ESG-related scores as
illustrative outcome-intensity proxies. The model is channel-specific, marginal, and preliminary. It
does not claim to capture engagement, stewardship, philanthropy, policy, every asset class, or a full
welfare analysis. Nor does it provide a final estimate of realized emissions reduction or social value.

The central conceptual message is that investor impact is an equilibrium contribution quantity, not
something that follows automatically from intention or from the outcomes attributed to firms.

- **Directly supported facts:**

- **Synthesis:**

paper explicitly says these are ‘returns’ only in the sense of outcome units per dollar; they are not
expected financial returns. The frontier compares them with certainty-equivalent financial return
costs without monetizing the outcome.

**In *Impact Returns: Three Case Studies***, expected investor impact is converted into an impact
value through a strategic price of impact. That value is divided by investment cost, and sometimes
annualized, producing a percentage designed to sit alongside a financial return for the investor’s
decision. The strategic price is contextual and should be informed by opportunity cost rather than
claimed as a market price or universal social value.

AskTPP should disambiguate the term every time there is a realistic risk of confusion, for example:

- ‘outcome-unit impact return (Impact Frontier definition)’
- ‘monetized strategic impact return (case-study definition)’

A future unified terminology may be possible, but it is not established by the current corpus.

## APP-02 — What are the most important things to know?

**Canonical question:** What are the most important things to know?

**Alternate phrasings**

- Give me the big picture.

**Audience:** All users

**Clarifying question, if needed:** I can give you a much more useful answer if I know where you are coming from. Are you approaching this as an asset owner or wealth holder, an advisor, a researcher, a CSP colleague or participant, a philanthropist, or someone exploring investor impact more generally?

**Suggested short answer**

Across audiences, five themes recur: clarify the goal; distinguish company impact from investor contribution; identify the mechanism and counterfactual; compare opportunities coherently across the whole portfolio; and treat quantitative estimates as uncertain, channel-specific inputs rather than automatic answers.

**Suggested fuller answer**

The emphasis should change by audience, but a cross-corpus synthesis is:

1. **Capital is a means, not the final goal.** Start with the outcomes, people, time horizon, risk, and
   degree of ambition that matter.
2. **Company impact is not investor impact.** Owning or financing an impactful enterprise does not
   by itself establish what changed because of the investor.
3. **Mechanisms and counterfactuals matter.** Capital provision, flexible terms, engagement,
   signaling, philanthropy, policy, and nonfinancial support work differently and require different
   evidence.
4. **Make decisions coherently across the total portfolio.** Compare investments, grants, structures,
   and other resources under consistent goals and opportunity costs rather than in silos.
5. **Systems and other forms of capital can matter.** Relationships, knowledge, reputation, policy,
   enabling conditions, and collaboration may be as important as financial allocation.
6. **Quantification is useful but conditional.** Contribution multipliers, impact returns, and frontiers

## APP-05 — How can I assess whether a particular investment has investor impact?

**Canonical question:** How can I assess whether a particular investment has investor impact?

**Alternate phrasings**

**Match priority:** High

**Answer mode:** Clarify opportunity details before assessing

**Clarifying question, if needed:** What is the investment, asset class, stage, size, outcome, holding period, and proposed mechanism of change? Are you a direct investor, fund investor, or secondary-market buyer?

**Suggested short answer**

Assess the expected real-world outcome, the counterfactual without the enterprise, the counterfactual without your capital or action, the mechanism connecting you to change, the scale and uncertainty of that change, and the financial and nonfinancial costs. **Suggested fuller answer**

1. **Outcome:** What specific social or environmental variable could change, for whom, by how much,

2. **Enterprise contribution:** What would happen without this enterprise or project? Are there

3. **Investor mechanism:** Is the proposed contribution capital provision, flexible terms,
   nonfinancial support, engagement, signaling, coalition action, or something else?
4. **Investor counterfactual:** What would happen without this investor? Would another investor,
   funder, or owner provide similar capital or influence?
5. **Scalability and constraints:** Can additional capital or support productively change activity now?
   What limits growth or improvement?
6. **System response:** Could other investors, firms, policy, prices, or stakeholders offset or amplify
   the action?
7. **Uncertainty and risk:** What probabilities, delays, unintended outcomes, and model errors matter?
8. **Cost and opportunity cost:** What financial return, risk, liquidity, fees, staff time, or alternative
   impact is forgone?
9. **Evidence and monitoring:** What would update the assessment after investment?

## APP-06 — How should I compare financial returns and impact?

**Canonical question:** How should I compare financial returns and impact?

**Alternate phrasings**
- Can I add impact return to financial return?
- How do I decide whether below-market returns are worth it?
- What is impact-adjusted return?
- How should opportunity cost enter impact investing?

**Audience:** Asset owners, philanthropists, advisors, analysts

**Why a user might ask:** The answer depends on which impact-return definition is being used and on the investor’s goals.

**Match priority:** High

**Answer mode:** Disambiguate the impact-return definition first

**Clarifying question, if needed:** Do you mean outcome-unit impact returns from *The Impact Frontier* or monetized strategic impact returns from the case studies? What goal, alternative opportunity, risk, and time horizon are you comparing?

**Suggested short answer**

First make the units and values explicit. Outcome-unit impact returns cannot be added directly to percentage financial returns. To make a combined comparison, an investor must supply a transparent value or strategic price for the outcome, grounded in their goals and opportunity costs, then apply it consistently and account for risk, timing, liquidity, and contribution.

**Suggested fuller answer**

There are two legitimate but distinct comparison routes.

**Frontier or cost-effectiveness route:** Keep impact in physical or outcome units. Compare the
certainty-equivalent financial cost required to produce a target amount of contribution-adjusted
outcome, or compare outcome produced at a fixed financial cost. This avoids monetizing moral or
social value, but it only compares opportunities producing commensurable outcomes or requires a
separate values vector.

**Strategic-pricing route:** Assign a decision-specific strategic price of impact based on what the
investor could achieve through the best relevant alternative they are genuinely willing to fund.
Convert expected investor contribution into an impact value and express it relative to investment
cost. A monetized impact return can then be considered alongside expected financial return, with
careful treatment of timing and risk.

In both routes:

- use investor contribution, not gross company impact;
- include uncertainty and downside outcomes;
- compare like with like on risk, duration, liquidity, and scale;
- use the same values across competing opportunities;
- examine total impact value as well as percentage return when fixed costs or scale matter; and
- state whose preferences or mandate determine the valuation.

A below-market return is not automatically justified by an impact label. The relevant question is
whether the expected additional impact is worth more, under the investor’s coherent goals and
opportunity set, than the financial and alternative-impact value being given up.

- **Directly supported facts:**

- **Editorial or institutional judgment requiring confirmation:**
  - The acceptability of monetizing different outcomes and the preferred strategic benchmark are investor or institutional judgments.

**Supporting sources and passages**

**Important cautions or prohibited claims**
- Never add outcome-unit impact returns directly to financial percentages. Do not use social cost as a strategic price without explaining why. Do not treat a subjective valuation as objective market data.

**Suggested short answer**

A public-equity tilt can serve values alignment, risk, signaling, or capital-allocation goals, but these are different claims. - **Engagement and stewardship:** use ownership rights, dialogue, voting, and escalation to seek
  company change.

stable, or that ESG scores measure marginal outcomes. The paper uses the scores illustratively and
notes substantial data and model limitations. A practical strategy should also compare engagement

## SCOPE-04 — What are the main limitations of The Impact Frontier?

**Match priority:** High

**Answer mode:** Direct answer

**Clarifying question, if needed:** Ask whether the user wants theoretical, empirical, outcome-measurement, portfolio, or implementation limitations.

**Suggested short answer**

The current paper is a marginal, channel-specific, public-equity calibration. It models capital allocation rather than all investor-impact channels; uses parsimonious supply and demand specifications; relies on difficult-to-measure marginal outcome intensities and illustrative ESG proxies; and is not a dynamic welfare model, realized-impact estimate, or ready-made strategy for a specific investor.

**Suggested fuller answer**

Important limitations include:

- **Channel scope:** the model studies capital allocation. Engagement, stewardship, governance,
  philanthropy, policy advocacy, nonfinancial support, and signaling require separate analysis.
- **Marginality:** it evaluates sustained marginal shifts around a benchmark. Large reallocations,
  coalitions, endogenous policy, and structural change may behave differently.
- **Market and horizon:** the current empirical calibration is for U.S. public equities using specified
  multi-year windows. Results are not automatically portable to private markets, debt, real assets,
  other countries, or other horizons.
- **Supply and demand specification:** the slope matrices are tractable approximations, not a complete
  model of every investor, issuer, capital-structure choice, primary issuance, repurchase, or dynamic
  adjustment.

  Familiar ESG or accounting measures are generally not marginal causal effects.

  risk-free holdings and other system assumptions.
- **Portfolio and values inputs:** opportunity sets, covariance or effective cost, investor values, and
  governance determine the relevant frontier.
- **Welfare and dynamics:** the calibration is not a full welfare analysis and does not endogenize all
  consumption, saving, labor, wealth, volatility, or capital-adjustment dynamics.
- **Empirical status:** the estimates are preliminary and model-dependent.

The model can still be valuable as a disciplined benchmark and research program. Its limitations
define the additional evidence needed for a real application.

**Suggested short answer**

**Suggested fuller answer**

paper uses MSCI Social, Environmental, Governance, and Carbon Emissions scores illustratively.

external outcomes.

demonstration. The claim must match the use.

## SCOPE-06 — Does a public-equity investment definitely create real-world impact?

**Canonical question:** Does a public-equity investment definitely create real-world impact?

**Alternate phrasings**
- Does buying shares change company behavior?
- Can secondary-market investing be additional?
- Is public-equity impact zero?
- Did The Impact Frontier prove that ESG investing works?

**Audience:** All users, especially investors and critics

**Why a user might ask:** The debate is often framed in false absolutes: every investment has impact versus secondary-market purchases can never matter.

**Match priority:** High

**Answer mode:** Direct answer

**Clarifying question, if needed:** What action, mechanism, firm, market, holding period, and counterfactual are being considered?

**Suggested short answer**

No definite conclusion follows from the asset-class label alone. A public-equity action may affect firms through capital allocation, engagement, signaling, governance, or wider market and policy channels, but the magnitude depends on conditions and may be small. *The Impact Frontier* estimates one marginal channel; it does not prove that every purchase or ESG fund causes material change.

**Suggested fuller answer**

Public-equity investor impact is a mechanism-and-evidence question.

A secondary-market transaction does not hand cash directly to the issuer in the moment, but sustained
changes in investor demand can affect prices, cost of capital, issuance, repurchases, investment, and
other investors’ positions. Whether those changes become real activity depends on investor-side and
issuer-side responses. *The Impact Frontier* formalizes that capital-allocation pathway and estimates
nonzero, heterogeneous contribution multipliers in its current calibration.

Other channels are distinct. Shareholder engagement and voting may influence operations or
governance. Divestment or screening may send market or public signals. Coalition and policy effects
may operate over longer horizons. The evidence and requirements differ by channel.

The correct answer is therefore neither ‘every investment has causal impact’ nor ‘public markets can
never matter’. It is: specify the action and counterfactual, identify the plausible mechanism, estimate
magnitude and uncertainty, and compare it with alternatives. A small individual purchase in a highly
liquid, well-financed company may have little capital-allocation contribution, while another action,
firm, or channel may have more.

The paper’s working estimates should not be generalized into a blanket endorsement of ESG funds or
public-equity impact claims.

**Evidence map**

- **Directly supported facts:**
  - PUB-IGI, mechanism framework and evidence table, supports conditionality across channels. PUB-IF, pp. 1–10 and 30–36, supports equilibrium capital allocation and caveats.
- **Synthesis:**
  - The rejection of both absolutes is synthesis.
- **Editorial or institutional judgment requiring confirmation:**
  - The threshold for describing an effect as ‘material’ is investor- and outcome-specific.

**Supporting sources and passages**
- PUB-IGI, pp. 12–27; PUB-IF, pp. 1–10 and 30–36.

**Unresolved uncertainties**
- Empirical evidence varies by market, action, time horizon, and outcome.

**Important cautions or prohibited claims**
- Do not say the paper proves ESG investing works. Do not say public-equity impact is always zero or always positive. Do not confuse temporary price pressure with lasting real outcomes.

# 12. Proposed matching and response behavior

A production matcher should consider canonical question, alternate phrasings, semantic similarity,
named entities, user audience, and requested level of detail. A match should be rejected when a
material qualifier changes the answer—for example, “current CSP structure” versus “what CSP was
when the 2020 guide was published,” or “impact return in the paper” versus “impact return in the case
studies.”

For a strong match:

1. Select the approved short or fuller answer.
2. Preserve mandatory caveats and prohibited-claim guards.
3. Retrieve the listed supporting passages.
4. Tailor examples, length, and terminology to the user without changing the approved proposition.
5. Identify preliminary or version-sensitive material.
6. Ask the stored clarifying question when the answer is audience- or context-dependent.
7. Cite the underlying publication rather than citing only the FAQ.

For a weak or partial match:

1. Do not force the closest FAQ answer.
2. Retrieve from the ordinary corpus.
3. State when the response is synthesis.
4. Avoid making institutional or authorship inferences.
5. Offer one targeted clarifying question when it would materially improve accuracy.
